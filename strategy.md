# BridgePath outreach strategy

**Decision memo · Fictional eight-week pilot · Independent portfolio exercise, November 2026**

## Recommendation

Allocate up to $1,440 to a referral-led pilot consisting of three partner activations, one $300 paid-search test and one print distribution round. Hold $480 of that allocation for learner support and spend outreach funds in stages. The model forecasts 17.74 first-session starts, with 53.73 hours of outreach and intake work.

Intake availability determines whether the full plan can proceed. Expected bookings reach 29.64 of 30 available appointments. A missed appointment still consumes staff time; a rescheduled appointment needs another available slot. The coordinator should pause outreach when the remaining calendar cannot support the next activity, even if cash remains.

This is a new exercise for a fictional program. None of the assumptions, materials or forecasts in this memo describe a campaign implemented by Mercy Education Project.

## The program and the problem

BridgePath is assumed to offer free adult basic education and GED preparation. It has instructional funding for 24 new learners, but limited time to answer inquiries, assess program fit and complete intake. Learners may need clarity about schedules, starting requirements and the first appointment before they can commit. These are hypotheses to investigate, not findings from learner interviews.

The eight-week objective is a first teaching session attended. GED completion, continued attendance and employment outcomes fall outside the model. Starts that occur after the pilot's final week should be recorded separately from the pilot outcome.

A planning ceiling of 18 expected starts leaves room below the 24-seat operational limit. It does not guarantee that an actual campaign will stay within either the appointment or seat limit. Confirmed bookings and seat reservations require individual calendar checks.

## Give each audience one useful next step

| Audience | Message to test | Primary next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prospective learner | Explain what is offered, when learning can happen and what the first conversation covers. | Request an intake conversation through a short form or telephone contact. |
| Referral partner | Provide a one-page service description, contact route and current capacity status. | Ask the learner's permission to introduce them to the coordinator. |
| Potential donor | Explain the service and the specific support needs the program can document. | Request an information conversation about supporting the program. |

Learner and donor messages should use separate pages or clearly separated sections. A prospective learner should not have to pass a donation request to find the intake route. Donor outreach has no modeled revenue target in this exercise. The cash ceiling is treated as already available.

### Sample learner copy

> Thinking about returning to learning? BridgePath offers free adult education and GED preparation. Tell us your learning goal and a good time to reach you. A coordinator will discuss the available schedule and the first appointment before you decide to begin.

The public call to action would be **Request an intake conversation**. A short form should ask for a name, a preferred contact method, a safe time to make contact and the broad learning goal. It should explain how the program will use the information. Detailed educational or personal histories belong in the intake process rather than an advertising form.

### Sample partner copy

> Know an adult who is considering GED preparation? With their permission, introduce them to the BridgePath coordinator. We will check program fit and appointment availability with the learner directly. Please confirm the current schedule before sharing a start date.

These drafts are examples for the fictional program. The exercise does not assert that partner relationships, a live form or paid campaigns already exist.

## Choose channels for both response and follow-up cost

All figures below are invented assumptions per outreach unit. The complete funnel inputs are in [scenario.json](data/scenario.json).

| Channel unit | Cash | Outreach hours before intake | Expected inquiries | Tradeoff to investigate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| One partner activation | $120 | 2.0 | 12 | Referrals may arrive with clearer expectations, but partner attendance assumptions drive the result. |
| One community information session | $350 | 6.0 | 18 | An event permits questions in person, with a larger fixed commitment of staff time and cash. |
| One paid-search test | $300 | 1.0 | 20 | Search creates another route into the program, but lower assumed fit means more triage per start. |
| One print distribution round | $100 | 2.5 | 8 | Print may reach people who miss online messages; source attribution needs a simple recorded code or question. |

The partner allocation covers small outreach materials and coordination costs. Event cash covers a modest session's space, materials and participant access costs. These are budget placeholders to replace with quotes before implementation. Staff hours are an existing allocation, not paid again through these cash lines.

The pilot caps the channels at three partner activations, two events, three search tests and two print rounds. Response is assumed linear only within those small caps. The model contains no evidence about saturation, overlapping audiences or the benefit of repeated exposure. Deduplicating inquiries is necessary to avoid counting one learner twice.

The selected mix favors partner referrals, with a search test and print round providing other paths to the service. The event-led alternative is only 0.28 expected starts lower. Its additional $30 allocation and 3.62 staff hours make the selected mix preferable under the base assumptions, but this small modeled difference is weak evidence for excluding events in practice.

## Protect the resources that make outreach useful

The $1,440 allocation reconciles to $760 of outreach, $200 of shared setup and $480 of learner support. The support reserve assumes $20 for each of 24 available seats. It is held even for seats that remain unfilled and is not assumed to be sufficient for every learner's needs. The remaining $60 stays unallocated.

Shared setup has six staff hours and a $200 cash allowance for a simple inquiry page and outreach materials. Each inquiry then requires 15 minutes of triage. Each booked intake uses 45 minutes whether the learner attends or misses it. Channel-specific outreach work adds 9.5 hours in the selected plan.

The staff calculation is therefore **6 setup + 9.5 outreach + 16 triage + 22.23 booked intake = 53.73 hours**. Its 6.27-hour remainder is a contingency for administration and unexpected follow-up. That spare staff time does not create extra appointment slots. The 30-slot calendar and the staff allocation are separate constraints.

Funded teaching time is outside this outreach budget. The model's allocated dollars per expected start must not be described as the total cost of educating a learner.

## Release the pilot in stages

| Timing | Work and owner | Release condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Program coordinator confirms funded seats and the intake calendar. Outreach lead prepares the learner page and partner brief. | Program description, contact handling and appointment ownership are clear. |
| Weeks 2–3 | Outreach lead activates the first partner and the capped search test. Coordinator triages inquiries and books available slots. | Every inquiry has a responsible person and a next action; bookings fit the remaining calendar. |
| Week 4 | Coordinator reviews actual inquiry quality, time spent and attendance with the outreach lead. | Revised assumptions support additional outreach without exceeding cash, staff or calendar limits. |
| Weeks 5–6 | Outreach lead activates up to two more partners and the print round. | Release each activity separately after checking capacity. A paused activity remains unspent. |
| Weeks 7–8 | Coordinator completes booked intakes and confirms first-session attendance. Outreach lead records results and unresolved follow-up. | Stop creating demand that cannot receive a timely intake within the pilot. |

The initial release assumes one partner activation and one search test. That smaller combination forecasts 12.60 bookings and 7.24 starts, leaving room to observe the actual response before expanding. A reasonable initial service target is to acknowledge a new inquiry within two working days. The team should revise that target if observed handling time makes it unrealistic.

The full-plan forecast assumes all selected activities run and their responses convert within eight weeks. Delays or paused activities will change the total. The release conditions are proposed operating rules, not evidence that this plan has been approved.

## Measure the handoffs and revisit the choice

Record a unique learner identifier, first recorded source, inquiry date, fit status, booking date, attendance and first teaching-session date. The source question should allow an unknown response. Assign subsequent contacts to the same learner rather than creating another inquiry. Record donor contacts separately.

Use each immediately preceding stage as the denominator for a conversion rate. For example, partner attendance is attended partner intakes divided by booked partner intakes. The start rate is first teaching sessions attended divided by attended intakes. Report counts next to percentages while the sample is small.

The model assumes at most one booked intake per inquiry. Actual reschedules or extra assessment visits require additional calendar slots and staff time; record them separately and update the model before releasing more outreach. The first teaching session uses a funded learner seat, not an intake slot.

If partner attendance changes from the assumed 80% to 60%, the original mix forecasts 14.29 starts. Re-optimizing produces a different allocation: two partner activations, one event and two print rounds, with 15.16 expected starts. Review appointment timing, reminder preferences and reasons for missed visits before interpreting that difference as a channel problem. A single small pilot cannot isolate a causal effect.

At the review, replace assumed inquiry volumes, stage conversion rates and actual handling time with observed values. Retain the data definitions, rerun the analysis and compare the new recommendation with available partner relationships and staff judgment. The [generated decision brief](results/decision-brief.md) provides the calculations for this starting scenario.
